# Honest limitations

- **This is descriptive statistics of past seismicity, not prediction.**
  Nothing here forecasts the timing, location, or size of the next
  earthquake. Any reading of these numbers as a forecast is a misuse.
- **Catalog completeness varies by magnitude and era.** M2.5 is a reasonable
  completeness level for this region in recent years, but smaller events are
  undercounted, and older or more remote parts of the region are less
  complete. Rates inferred from a ~10-year window are approximate and
  dominated by the biggest sequences.
- The aftershock selection is a **simple distance/time box**, not a proper
  declustering algorithm — some background events are mixed in, and the
  fitted c/p values would shift with a stricter declustering.
- "Distance to Seattle" is **epicentral distance**, not shaking intensity,
  which also depends on depth, local geology, and building type. Do not read
  the 200 km count as "314 dangerous events near Seattle."
- The least-squares b-value (0.835) differs from the MLE (1.027) because
  least squares on cumulative counts is pulled by the largest events; the
  MLE is the trustworthy estimate and the one quoted in the briefing.
- The ~10-year window is short relative to Cascadia's ~500-year megathrust
  cycle. This catalog slice characterizes *background and crustal*
  seismicity, not the subduction-zone hazard that dominates long-term risk.
