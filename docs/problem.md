# Problem statement

The Cascadia region faces a major earthquake in the coming decades. The
Cascadia subduction zone has produced magnitude 9-class events on a roughly
500-year rhythm, and the last one was in 1700. Seattle, Portland, and the
surrounding region sit on top of that hazard.

Households and cities need **honest, data-grounded preparedness**: what does
the recent seismic record actually show, communicated without hype and without
pretending we can forecast what comes next?

This project answers the descriptive questions well:

- How many earthquakes has the Pacific Northwest actually had in the last decade?
- What does the magnitude-frequency distribution look like (Gutenberg–Richter b-value)?
- When a large event happens, how does its aftershock sequence decay (Omori–Utsu)?
- How many of those events were close to where people live?

It does **not** answer "when is the next big one" — no method here has
forecasting power, and the output says so repeatedly. See
[limitations.md](limitations.md).
