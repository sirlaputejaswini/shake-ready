SHAKE-READY: PACIFIC NORTHWEST SEISMICITY BRIEFING
Generated 2026-09-18 from the USGS Earthquake Catalog (public domain).

IMPORTANT: Everything in this briefing describes PAST seismicity. It does not predict when, where, or how large the next earthquake will be. No statistical method applied here has forecasting power.

WHAT THE DATA SHOW (2016 to present, magnitude 2.5 and up):
- 3,463 earthquakes were recorded in the Pacific Northwest bounding box between 2016-01-01 and 2026-09-16.
- The catalog is reasonably complete down to magnitude 2.5.
- Gutenberg-Richter b-value: 1.03 (least-squares check: 0.83). A b-value near 1 is typical worldwide: each step down in magnitude brings roughly ten times as many earthquakes.
- Largest event in the window: magnitude 7.0, 2024 Offshore Cape Mendocino, California Earthquake on 2024-12-05.
- After that mainshock, 322 aftershocks were recorded within 100 km over 30 days. The decay fits the Omori-Utsu law with p = 1.18 (c = 0.785 days, R^2 = 0.88) — a textbook relaxing sequence, exactly what seismologists expect.
- 314 of the events occurred within 200 km of downtown Seattle; the median event depth was 12.5 km.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR PREPAREDNESS:
- The Pacific Northwest is seismically active on human timescales: hundreds of detectable earthquakes every year. Most are too small to feel, but the same faults produce the region's large ones.
- Aftershock sequences last weeks to months after a large event — that is the one timing statement the data support, and it only applies *after* a mainshock has already happened.
- Standard public guidance (not derived from this data): keep two weeks of food, medication, and emergency supplies; secure heavy furniture; know Drop, Cover, and Hold On; keep shoes and a flashlight by the bed. See ready.gov and the Washington Emergency Management Division for checklists.

HONEST LIMITS OF THIS BRIEFING:
- Magnitude 2.5 is a *recent-era* completeness level for this region. Earlier catalogs and smaller events are undercounted, so long-term rates inferred from short windows are approximate.
- The aftershock box is a simple distance/time window, not a proper declustering — a few background events are mixed in.
- Seattle is at (47.6062, -122.3321); 'distance to Seattle' is epicentral distance, not shaking intensity, which depends on depth, geology, and building type.
- Again: none of this predicts the next earthquake.
